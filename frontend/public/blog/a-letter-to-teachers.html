A letter to the teachers who hold space.

I have five children who all, separately, have been carried through a hard week by a teacher who simply noticed them. I want to begin with that.

I know how much of your work is invisible. The corner of the classroom you arrange so the child who can’t sit still doesn’t have to. The two minutes you take at the door, every day, with the kid who needs to be greeted by name before anything can begin. The book you slipped onto a desk because you suspected the child needed it before the child knew they did.

You are the librarians and the listeners. You are the room. Thank you for the small, exhausting, world-shaping things that you do that nobody writes about.

If a book of mine ends up on your classroom shelf, that is more than enough for me. If a child of yours hands it back and says only, “that one’s mine now” — please tell them I am very glad they found it.
