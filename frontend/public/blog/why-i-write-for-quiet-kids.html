Why I write for the quiet kids.

This is the letter I would have wanted to find in a library when I was eleven.

You know who you are. You replay the lunch-time conversation on the bus home. You notice the new kid before anyone else does. You can tell, by the angle of your mother’s shoulders, what kind of evening it is going to be. You read faces the way other people read time on a watch — quickly, automatically, without meaning to.

The world has a habit of telling kids like you to “relax” or “stop overthinking it.” I want to tell you something different. Your attention is not a malfunction. It is a kind of skill, and one day, in a kitchen, on a stage, in a quiet office, or in a noisy classroom, somebody is going to need it very badly. And you will know exactly what to say.

Until then: please be patient with the part of you that notices. It’s the part of you I am writing for.
