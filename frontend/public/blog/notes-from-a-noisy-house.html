Notes from a noisy house.

People often ask how I write — and what they usually mean is, how do you write at all, with five of them. The honest answer is that the noise is the material. It would be a much worse book if the house were silent.

I write in the first hour after the dog and before the children. I write for fifteen minutes at the kitchen island while the kettle boils. I write a single sentence on the back of an envelope when one of the kids says something that I know — the way you know a bird is about to land before it does — is going to end up in a book.

There is no calm version of this work. There is only the version where you let the noise in and try to put it in the right order.

If you are also writing in small slivers between other lives, this letter is for you. Don’t wait for the quiet hour. It is not coming.
